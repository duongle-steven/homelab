    Step 15e: supply chain

    Status: designed in chat 2026-10-29. Branch: feat/supply-chain from dev. Decisions marked (user) were made in chat. The rest are (Claude's call).

    Goal

    What runs in the cluster is known and watched:

    • an image with a fixable critical CVE, or a secret baked into an image, raises an alert;
    • our own image (lab's) is signed by the workflow that built it, with its SBOM attached, and Kyverno checks that signature;
    • the GitHub Actions workflows that build and sign it are checked for known weaknesses on every pull request.

    What exists (measured 2026-10-29)

    • 15a's images check scans only the images new in a pull request's renders. Nothing watches the images already running when a CVE is published later.
    • lab's image is built by .github/workflows/lab-image.yaml from master and pushed to ghcr.io/duongle-steven/homelab-lab:<sha>. platform/secrets-rotation/values.yaml pins its digest by hand. It is neither signed nor shipped with an SBOM.
    • The blog's image (registry.<domain>/blog:latest) has no pipeline yet.
    • Kyverno 1.19 runs in Audit; Enforce is 15b-2.
    • zizmor 1.31.1 (offline) finds 14 issues in .github/: 10 suppressed, 4 low, none medium or high.

    Scope (user)

    • Trivy Operator scans only what nothing else covers.
    • It scans image vulnerabilities and exposed secrets in images.
    • Config audit is off: Kyverno covers it (15b).
    • RBAC assessment, infra assessment and cluster compliance are off: kube-bench and kubescape cover them (15d).
    • The blog's signature waits for its pipeline (Woodpecker, org ci, a later step). That pipeline signs with a key in OpenBao. This step only records it in CLAUDE.md.

    Design (Claude's calls)

    1. Trivy Operator (system/trivy-operator)

    Version and setup - The trivy-operator chart at its latest release, from Aqua's repository. - One namespace trivy-operator (the directory name, as every component), inside the mesh. Its scan Jobs are short pods; they get the mesh's defaults. - ClientServer mode with the built-in Trivy server: the vulnerability DB is downloaded once and kept on the server.

    Scanners - vulnerabilityScannerEnabled and exposedSecretScannerEnabled are on. - configAuditScannerEnabled, rbacAssessmentScannerEnabled, infraAssessmentScannerEnabled, clusterComplianceEnabled and sbomGenerationEnabled are off. - Cluster SBOM reports would grow etcd, one CRD object per image. Our own image's SBOM is in step 2.

    Severity (user: unfixed findings are reported too, since they cannot be patched and stay exploitable) - Every finding is reported, fixed or not (ignoreUnfixed: false), at HIGH,CRITICAL. - The "Security" dashboard shows them all. - An unfixed CVE cannot be fixed by a bump. It is handled by one of these: - what already limits exploitation: default-deny egress, mTLS and deny-all, Pod Security, and 15g later; - replacing the app or turning off the vulnerable feature; - an acceptance with a reason and an expiry.

    Accepted findings - Accepted findings go in the operator's ignore policy in values.yaml. Each one carries a reason and an expiry, like 15a's .trivyignore.yaml. - An expired acceptance no longer applies, so the alert fires again. - The baseline is measured in the sandbox and reported in chat before it is accepted: - a fixable finding is fixed by bumping the image; - an unfixed CRITICAL is accepted with its reason, or its app is replaced.

    Metrics and alerts - Prometheus scrapes the operator through a ServiceMonitor. - Three PrometheusRule alerts, all warning and for: 1h, labelled by namespace, workload and image: - ImageCriticalVulnerability fires when a running image has a CRITICAL finding with a fix. The fix is a bump. - ImageCriticalVulnerabilityUnfixed fires when a running image has a CRITICAL finding with no fix and no acceptance. The fix is a decision: accept it with a reason and an expiry, or replace the app. - ImageExposedSecret fires when a secret is found in a running image. - HIGH findings alert on nothing and show only on the dashboard: base images carry many, and alerting on them would bury the alerts that matter. - Both reach ntfy and Telegram like every alert.

    Sandbox: 12GB, already tight once Kiali and Kyverno joined - One scan Job at a time (scanJobsConcurrentLimit: 1) with memory limits. - Images are pulled through the sandbox's pull-through caches (Trivy's registry mirrors), not from the internet again. - If the suite's memory still does not fit, the sandbox's scans are scheduled outside the suite. That decision is made in the plan's sandbox task, ledgered.

    Network - Egress 443 for trivy-operator (the DB and registries) in system/egress/values.yaml. - An allow AuthorizationPolicy for its namespace. - homelab/backup: none on any claim. - Pod Security restricted, or a PolicyException with its reason. - An entry in test/scopes.yaml.

    2. lab's image: signature and SBOM

    lab-image.yaml after the push - Generate an SPDX SBOM with Syft, pinned. - Sign the image by digest with cosign, keyless: GitHub's OIDC token, Sigstore's Fulcio and Rekor. - Attest the SBOM to the image (cosign attest --type spdxjson). - Permissions gain id-token: write. No key is kept, so cli/internal/rotate/catalog.yaml gets no new secret. - Actions are pinned by digest, as today.

    Kyverno policy, in system/kyverno, production only - An ImageValidatingPolicy matching ghcr.io/duongle-steven/homelab-lab*. - It requires: - a keyless signature from issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com; - subject https://github.com/duongle-steven/homelab/.github/workflows/lab-image.yaml@refs/heads/master; - the SPDX attestation from the same identity. - In Audit, like every policy until 15b-2. - Kyverno gets egress 443 to ghcr.io and Sigstore's TUF and Rekor.

    Sandbox - The sandbox runs lab's image built from the checkout, which is unsigned, so the production policy is not rendered there (values-dev.yaml). - TestSupplyChain proves Kyverno's verification path instead: - The test signs a small image in zot with a key pair it generates. - It creates its own Audit ImageValidatingPolicy for that image's repository, with that public key. - It runs a pod from the signed image, then one from an unsigned image in the same repository. Kyverno reads zot with the test's pull credentials (the policy's credentials). - It expects a pass report for the first and a fail report for the second. - The production policy is checked offline: it renders, with the identity fields above (TestLabImagePolicy). kyverno apply cannot check it offline, since verification fetches the image. - Its real verification against ghcr is listed under "Before the first production deploy". It needs the first signed release from master.

    3. zizmor in the basic checks

    • zizmor comes from the dev shell: unstable.zizmor 1.31.1, the latest.
    • A new check workflows in scripts/ci-check, and a job in checks.yaml's matrix that feeds the required checks.
    • It runs zizmor --offline .github/.
    • Any finding fails it. The 4 low findings today are fixed, or ignored in .github/zizmor.yml with a reason.
    • Online audits (they call GitHub's API) are not run: a token in the basic checks is not worth what they find here.

    Tests

    • TestTrivyOperatorRender (offline) checks:
    • only the two scanners are on;
    • ignoreUnfixed is false;
    • every ignore entry has a reason and an unexpired expiry;
    • the alerts' expressions use metrics the operator exports.
    • TestTrivyOperator (sandbox):
    • the operator is running;
    • a VulnerabilityReport appears for a known workload;
    • an image with a known fixable CRITICAL CVE, run in a test namespace, raises ImageCriticalVulnerability's expression in Prometheus;
    • after the suite, no other workload has an unaccepted CRITICAL finding, fixed or not.
    • TestSupplyChain (sandbox): the signed and unsigned pods, as in section 2.
    • TestLabImagePolicy (offline): the production policy with the identity above; the dev render has no policy.
    • The workflows check: a fixture workflow with a known finding (pull_request_target checking out the head) fails, and today's .github/ passes.

    Out of scope

    • The blog's pipeline and signature (later, with its repository).
    • Signing or verifying third-party images.
    • Enforce (15b-2).
    • Trivy's config, RBAC and compliance scans (15b and 15d).
